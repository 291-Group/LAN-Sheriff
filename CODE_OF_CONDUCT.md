# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Assume they meant well. Disagree about the work rather than
about each other.

This project is a network monitor that people run on their own homes and
offices, and the questions it attracts are often ones somebody is slightly
embarrassed to ask: what is this device on my network, why is my television
talking to Korea, is this normal. Nobody should have to be an expert before they
are allowed to ask.

## What is expected

- Treat questions as reasonable, including the beginner ones. Everyone was new.
- Criticise ideas, code and decisions. Not the person who had them.
- Accept that other people run different hardware, different networks and
  different lives, and that a thing which is obvious on your setup may be
  genuinely puzzling on theirs.
- When you are wrong, say so plainly and move on. It costs nothing.

## What is not

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks, in issues, pull requests, commit
  messages or anywhere else this project's name is attached.
- Sexual language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Publishing anyone's private information. This is worth stating twice in a
  project like this one: **network data is personal.** Addresses, device names,
  the places a household connects to and the times it does so all describe the
  people living there. Do not paste somebody's real capture into an issue, and
  redact your own before you do.
- Sustained disruption: reopening settled arguments, demanding unpaid work, or
  treating maintainers as a support contract.

## Scope

This applies in the repository, in issues and pull requests, and anywhere
somebody is representing the project.

## Reporting

Mail **info@291group.com**. Reports are read by the maintainers and kept private.

Say what happened, where, and roughly when. You do not need to prove anything or
argue your case; a link and a sentence is enough.

We will respond, and we will tell you what we did. If your report concerns a
maintainer, say so, and it will be handled by someone else.

## What happens then

Depending on what happened and whether it continues: a private word, a public
note that a line was crossed, removal of the offending content, or a ban from
the project. We would much rather use the first one.

Enforcement is a judgement call made by people, not a formula. Where it is a
close call, the tie goes to the person who felt unwelcome, because they are the
one with less power in the situation and the more to lose by speaking up.
